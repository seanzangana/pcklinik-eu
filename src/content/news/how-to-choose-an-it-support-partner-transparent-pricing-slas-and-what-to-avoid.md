Finding the right IT support provider can feel overwhelming. There are countless companies out there, each promising the best service, the fastest response times, and the most competitive pricing. Yet many business owners end up disappointed after signing a contract, only to discover hidden costs, vague service commitments, or support that simply doesn't match their needs.

The good news is that choosing an IT support provider selection doesn't have to be complicated. When you know what to look for—and what to avoid—you can make a decision that actually serves your business. This guide walks you through the key factors: transparent pricing models, service-level agreements, certifications that matter, and the red flags that should make you walk away.

## Understanding Your IT Support Needs First

Before you even start looking at IT support providers, you need to understand what your business actually requires. Too many companies skip this step and end up buying more—or less—support than they need.

Start by asking yourself a few basic questions. How many employees do you have? What's your industry, and does it have specific compliance requirements? How critical is your IT infrastructure to your daily operations? If your systems go down, how long can your business tolerate that downtime?

A small accounting firm with 8 employees has completely different needs than a manufacturing company with 60 employees and specialized machinery running on networked systems. One might need basic email and printer support, while the other requires 24/7 monitoring and rapid response to system failures.

Write down your current pain points. Are you frustrated by slow response times? Do you feel like you're thrown from one support person to another without continuity? Are you worried about security? These real frustrations should guide your IT support provider selection. The right provider should be able to address your specific challenges, not just offer generic support packages.

## Transparent Pricing Models: Pay-Per-Incident vs. Fixed-Price Support

Pricing is often where IT support relationships fall apart. You think you know what you're paying for, then unexpected costs show up on your invoice. Understanding the different pricing models available helps you avoid this trap.

### Pay-Per-Incident Support

With pay-per-incident pricing, you pay for support only when you request it. On the surface, this sounds attractive—you only pay when you need help.

The reality is more complicated. A single support incident might cost 1,500 DKK to 3,000 DKK depending on the complexity and how long it takes to resolve. If you have 5 employees and someone's computer stops working twice a month, you could be looking at 15,000 DKK to 30,000 DKK per month in unpredictable costs. Plus, businesses often hesitate to call support when they know they're paying per incident, which means problems get worse before they get fixed.

Pay-per-incident also doesn't encourage preventive maintenance. Your IT support provider has no incentive to prevent problems—they make more money when things break.

### Fixed-Price Support (Managed IT Services)

Fixed-price IT support, often called managed IT services, works like a subscription. You pay a flat fee each month, typically between 3,000 DKK and 8,000 DKK per user per month depending on the scope of services and your industry.

This model is dramatically different. Your provider now has incentive to keep your systems running smoothly. Fewer problems mean lower costs for them, so they focus on prevention, monitoring, and maintenance. You also get predictable budgeting—you know exactly what your IT support will cost each month.

Most managed IT service contracts include a defined set of services: monitoring and alerting, software updates, security patches, helpdesk support during business hours (or 24/7), network management, and backup and disaster recovery.

### What Real Transparency Looks Like

Regardless of the pricing model, a transparent IT support provider will give you a clear written breakdown of what's included and what costs extra. You should never be surprised by an invoice.

Ask these specific questions before signing any contract:

- What's included in the base price, and what incurs additional charges?

- How is each component priced (per user, per device, per month)?

- If something goes wrong, will there be extra fees for emergency support or after-hours response?

- Are there separate charges for specific services like security assessments, data recovery, or hardware replacement?

- If my business grows by 10 employees, how does the pricing scale?

- What happens if I need to cancel—is there a penalty, and how much notice do I need to give?

The provider should be able to answer every one of these questions without hesitation. If they seem vague or keep saying 'we'll get back to you on that,' that's a warning sign.

## Service-Level Agreements: The Contract That Actually Matters

A service-level agreement (SLA) is your insurance policy. It spells out exactly what the provider promises to deliver and what happens if they don't.

Many business owners sign IT support contracts without really understanding the SLA, if one even exists. This is a mistake. The SLA is the only thing that protects you if service is poor.

### Response Time vs. Resolution Time

Here's a critical distinction that catches many businesses off guard: response time is not the same as resolution time.

Response time is how quickly the provider acknowledges your support request and gets someone working on it. A typical SLA might promise response within 4 business hours during normal business hours.

Resolution time is how long it actually takes to fix the problem. This varies wildly depending on the issue. A simple password reset might take 10 minutes. A network outage might take 8 hours. A corrupted server drive might take 2 days.

A good IT support provider will differentiate between different types of issues. For example, they might commit to:

- Critical issues (system down, no internet): 1-hour response, 4-hour resolution target

- High priority (major feature not working): 2-hour response, 8-hour resolution target

- Medium priority (minor annoyance, work-around available): 4-hour response, 24-hour resolution target

- Low priority (cosmetic issue, no business impact): best effort within business hours

This tiered approach is realistic and fair. Don't trust a provider that promises 1-hour resolution for everything—that's simply not realistic.

### What Happens When They Miss the SLA?

Many IT support providers include an SLA but never mention what happens when they miss it. If they miss their commitment, what's the consequence? Usually, there should be one.

Some providers offer service credits—a refund of 5% to 10% of that month's service fee if they miss their SLA commitments. Others might provide additional free support hours or a discount on the next month.

If your provider doesn't mention SLA credits or remedies, push back. They should stand behind their promises. And any credit or remedy should be automatic—you shouldn't have to fight them to get it.

Also check: Does the SLA apply to scheduled maintenance windows? Most providers exclude planned maintenance from their SLA commitments, which is reasonable. But you want to know this upfront.

## Certifications and Security Standards That Actually Matter

You'll see IT support providers throw around certifications like confetti. Some of these matter deeply for your security and compliance. Others are mostly marketing.

### ISAE 3402 Certification

ISAE 3402 (previously known as SAS 70) is an international auditing standard. If your IT support provider handles sensitive data—financial records, customer information, personnel files—they should have ISAE 3402 Type II certification.

Type II certification means an independent auditor has verified their security controls and controls over time. It's not a guarantee that nothing bad will happen, but it's strong evidence that they take security seriously and have solid processes in place.

GDPR compliance is another non-negotiable requirement if you store any personal data about EU citizens, which almost every Danish business does. Your IT support provider must be able to confirm GDPR compliance in writing.

### ISO 27001 Certification

ISO 27001 is an international standard for information security management. A provider with ISO 27001 certification has demonstrated that they maintain documented information security policies and procedures.

Again, this isn't a magic bullet, but it's evidence of a mature, audited security posture.

### Microsoft Partnership Status

If your business uses Microsoft 365, Active Directory, or other Microsoft products, it's worth knowing whether your IT support provider is a Microsoft partner. Not all support providers are. A Microsoft partner has demonstrated expertise with Microsoft technologies and stays current with updates and best practices.

Look specifically for Microsoft Gold Competency or Silver Competency status. These require ongoing training, certification of staff, and customer satisfaction ratings. They mean your provider is incentivized to stay current with Microsoft technologies.

### Don't Get Fooled

Some certifications are less meaningful. Vendor-specific training courses are valuable, but they're not the same as independent audited certifications. A salesperson having CompTIA A+ certification is nice but doesn't tell you much about the company's overall security practices.

Ask your potential providers for copies of their certifications and the dates they were earned. If they can't produce documentation, be skeptical. And verify independently if you can—certification bodies publish lists of certified companies.

## Red Flags to Avoid When Selecting an IT Support Provider

Some warning signs should make you walk away from an IT support provider selection before you even sign the contract.

### Vague or Missing SLAs

If a provider can't give you a clear, written SLA with specific response and resolution times, that's a problem. Same goes if they promise everything will be fixed immediately—that's not realistic and shows they're not being honest about their capabilities.

### Long-Term Lock-in Contracts

Avoid multi-year contracts with penalties if you leave early. A confident IT support provider doesn't need to lock you in for 3 years. If they're good, you'll want to stay. If they require 24-36 month contracts with 6-month penalty clauses, they're betting that you won't be satisfied but hoping you'll stay anyway because leaving is too expensive.

Look for 12-month contracts with 30-day cancellation clauses. This is fair to both parties and shows confidence in their service.

### Hidden Setup or Changeover Fees

Some providers quote a low monthly rate but then charge 10,000 DKK to 20,000 DKK in setup fees to get your systems integrated. Others charge fees every time they perform routine maintenance tasks. These hidden costs add up fast.

Ask specifically: What are all the fees I'll pay in the first 90 days? Get a written quote that includes setup costs.

### No References or Poor Online Reputation

Legitimate IT support providers can point to satisfied customers who'll speak positively about their service. If you can't find any case studies, testimonials, or positive reviews, that's suspicious.

Search online. Read reviews on independent review sites, not just their own website. Look for patterns. One negative review could be an outlier. Multiple complaints about the same issue—slow response times, lack of communication, poor follow-up—suggest a systemic problem.

### Support Staff Can't Explain Their Services

During your initial consultation, ask technical questions. Not super advanced stuff, but reasonable questions about how they'd approach your specific needs. If the person explaining their services can't articulate how they'd solve your problems, that suggests you'll have similar communication issues when you're actually paying them.

### Unwillingness to Discuss Pricing Details

Beware of providers who are cagey about costs or insist on a sales call before giving any pricing information. Legitimate providers can explain their pricing structure upfront. Yes, they might want a call to customize a quote for your specific situation, but they shouldn't be vague about how they charge or what's included.

### No Backup or Disaster Recovery Plan

Ask directly: If your servers go down, what's your backup? How often are backups created? Where are they stored? How long would it take to restore? If they seem unsure about the answer, that's concerning. Backup and disaster recovery should be a core part of any managed IT support offering.

## Questions to Ask Before Making Your Final Decision

Create a shortlist of 2-3 IT support providers based on pricing, certifications, and SLAs. Then ask each of them these questions before making your final choice:

- Can you walk me through exactly how I'd contact support and what happens after I submit a ticket?

- Who specifically will be managing my account, and how often will I hear from them?

- How do you handle 24/7 support if I need it? Do I switch to a different team outside business hours?

- If we discover a security issue, what's your process for notifying me and fixing it?

- Can you provide references from 3 customers similar in size and industry to my business?

- What's your staff turnover rate, and how do you ensure knowledge continuity if my account manager leaves?

- How do you stay current with new technologies and security threats?

- If my needs change, can we adjust the service level and pricing, and how quickly?

- What happens in a dispute? Do you have a conflict resolution process?

Their answers will reveal a lot about how seriously they take customer relationships and whether they're actually prepared to support your business or just selling a service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I expect to spend on IT support?

This depends heavily on your business size, industry, and complexity. For a small business with 10-15 employees and basic IT needs, expect 4,000 DKK to 6,000 DKK per month for managed IT services. For a 30-person company with more complex requirements, 8,000 DKK to 15,000 DKK per month is typical. Highly specialized industries or businesses with strict compliance requirements might pay 20,000 DKK or more monthly. Get quotes from multiple providers based on your specific needs rather than expecting a one-size-fits-all number.

### Should I choose a large national provider or a local IT support company?

Both have advantages. Large providers often have more resources, broader expertise, and stronger certifications. Local providers might be more responsive, understand your specific market better, and offer more personalized service. Neither is universally better—it depends on your priorities and the specific companies involved. Get proposals from both types and compare based on SLAs, pricing transparency, and how well they seem to understand your business.

### What if my current IT support provider isn't meeting expectations?

First, document the specific problems: missed SLAs, poor communication, unresolved issues. Send a formal letter requesting improvement with a 30-day timeframe. If nothing changes, initiate the cancellation process according to your contract. Then start your search for a new provider. Most IT support providers can handle a smooth transition from your current provider, though it may require 2-4 weeks of overlap to ensure continuity.

### Do I need 24/7 support, or is business hours support enough?

Most small to mid-sized Danish businesses operate during standard business hours (7 AM to 5 PM) and can function with business-hours support during the week and weekend coverage on standby. However, if you operate across time zones, have significant international clients, or run processes at night or on weekends, 24/7 support may be justified. Check your actual downtime incidents from the past year—if most issues happen during business hours, you probably don't need to pay for 24/7 coverage.

## Conclusion

Choosing an IT support provider selection is one of the most important technology decisions you'll make. The wrong choice wastes money and creates frustration. The right choice keeps your systems running, protects your data, and lets your team focus on business instead of troubleshooting.

The key is knowing what to look for. Transparent pricing that's explained clearly, service-level agreements with teeth, relevant certifications, and realistic promises matter far more than flashy marketing. And the red flags—vague contracts, hidden costs, poor references, inflexible terms—should stop you before you sign.

Start by understanding your actual needs, not what a salesperson says you should need. Then compare IT support provider selection options based on the criteria covered here. Get multiple quotes. Ask tough questions. Call references. Take your time with the decision.

A good IT support partnership lasts years. It's worth getting right from the start.
